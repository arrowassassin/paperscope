# Consumers buy outcomes, not capability shelves

The safe, cross-host, one-click AI skills store does not survive validation as a consumer business. The install-friction premise is real, but it is a developer complaint: consumers are blocked by trust and by payoff, not by JSON, and when asked what they want next, **43% name image creation and 40% writing help** while bills and inbox automation rank "near the bottom" of stated intent ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Worse for the thesis, every layer of the store was captured or abandoned in 2026 — Claude ships an "Add" button, Docker ships signed cross-client containers, Vercel's skills.sh indexes 60,000 skills across roughly 48 agent surfaces, and Smithery, the best-known independent one-click layer, shut its connection service on 31 October 2026 ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14328846-browse-skills-connectors-and-plugins-in-one-directory); [Smithery](https://smithery.ai/future)). The "safe" differentiator is the weakest part of the pitch: a static badge cannot certify adversarial, runtime-mutable software, and independent testers have already bypassed all three scanners on the leading cross-agent installer ([Kipt](https://kipt.me/guides/agent-skills-marketplaces)). Re-ranked against mass-appeal criteria rather than store logic, the three concepts worth building are a **local-first private document assistant**, an **AI operator for micro-businesses**, and a **creator artifact studio** — in that order, and only the first is structurally hard for a platform owner to copy. The unifying rule behind the ranking is blunt: in consumer AI, money is either bundled by a host or earned back by the user, and a horizontal shelf of capabilities is neither.

## Consumers want a finished thing, not a shelf of capabilities

The most important corrective to the store thesis is that demand runs from *thinking and producing* toward *acting inside accounts*, and most of the market is still on the first side. **64% of U.S. adults used AI in 2026, but only 25% daily**, and the single most common activity is learning or research at **26%** ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Writing support has crossed into majority use at **61%** of people who write, image creation at **53%**, presentation building at **52%**. Email is used for drafting (**47%**) and editing (**44%**) — text generation, not triage, not sending. Only **41%** of consumers have tried an agent at all and **24%** use one regularly. A store whose landing page leads with Gmail, Drive and Calendar connectors is selling the second act to an audience still in the first.

Stated intent makes the mismatch sharper, because the low-penetration jobs are not latent demand. Just **23%** of bill-payers use AI for bills and **25%** for staying in touch, and when asked what they would consider next, **43% said image creation and 40% writing help**, with bills and inbox chores at the bottom ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). The one genuine action category is shopping, where **50% of AI-using shoppers compared prices, 37% made a purchase decision and 26% let AI complete a purchase** — but the delegated act is *deciding*, not *connecting an account*. The viable consumer skills, in other words, are content-and-judgment skills, not integration skills.

The claimed blocker is also misdiagnosed. When choosing an AI tool, consumers now rank **accuracy (45%), trustworthiness (40%) and security/privacy (36%) above ease of use (32%)**, and ease of use *fell* from 38% the year before ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Among people who stick to general assistants, only **11%** are unaware that specialized tools exist; **27%** cite privacy concerns with lesser-known tools and **21%** "don't know which tool to trust." Small-business owners are blunter: **half named data security as their single biggest hesitation** about AI ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-small-business)). And **55% of 6,000+ consumers surveyed are "extra cautious about sharing payment information with AI"** ([Bloomberg via LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2026-03-30/chatgpt-app-store-falters-six-months-after-launch)). One-click install solves a problem consumers mostly do not have — developers are blocked by config files, consumers by "is this safe, and is it better than the app I already have?"

Discovery compounds the problem, because a store is fundamentally a discovery product. Friends and family drive **38%** of app discovery and win **84%** trust; app-store browsing drives **29%** and wins **73%**; AI tools are cited by roughly one in ten consumers and trusted by **44%**, the lowest of any channel ([Yodel Mobile / NP Digital](https://adtechtoday.com/research-ai-influences-app-discovery-but-personal-recommendations-remain-twice-as-trusted)). The category's own growth record confirms where distribution actually comes from: Gemini went from **36% to 58%** of AI users because Google put it inside Search, Android and Workspace, while Claude nearly tripled **without** built-in distribution by being deeper, not broader ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). A standalone store would be a browse-and-install product in the one channel consumers use least and trust least, competing against default placement.

## The install layer closed in 2026, and the protocol closed with it

The store's two differentiators — one-click and cross-host — were both converted into platform features within ten months. Anthropic shipped a **unified directory for skills, connectors and plugins on 25 September 2026**: browse, click "Add," the skill is enabled by default and view-only so installed contents cannot be silently modified ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14328846-browse-skills-connectors-and-plugins-in-one-directory)). ChatGPT's directory grew from **300+ integrations in March to 16,185 listings by 18 November** on OpenApps' count, at roughly 460 new apps a week, with no published install or revenue data ([OpenApps](https://theopenapps.com/stats/chatgpt); [Bloomberg via LA Times](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2026-03-30/chatgpt-app-store-falters-six-months-after-launch)). Docker went further than either, shipping **300+ verified servers as signed container images with SBOMs**, one-click enablement, OAuth, and a gateway that wires a single profile into Claude, Cursor, VS Code, Windsurf and Continue ([Docker docs](https://docs.docker.com/ai/mcp-catalog-and-toolkit/)). Vercel's `skills.sh` already does the cross-agent version with **60,000+ skills across roughly 48 agent surfaces** and public Gen/Socket/Snyk audits on every skill ([Kipt](https://kipt.me/guides/agent-skills-marketplaces)).

Demand that does reach a client gets captured by that client. Cline's in-client marketplace recorded **1.47 million installs across 199 servers, with 52% of installs in the top 10** ([hedwigai](https://www.hedwigai.com/blog/mcp-registries)). That distribution is the whole story of a store: a top-10 concentration means users want a handful of utilities, not a catalog, and the client that can execute the install keeps the relationship. The independent layer proved the point by exiting. Smithery, which built its brand on "finding and running an MCP server should take one click," was acquired by Arcade.dev on 5 August 2026 and **shut down its proxy, managed identity, API, CLI and SDK on 31 October 2026**, refunding every charge since the acquisition ([Smithery](https://smithery.ai/future); [Arcade.dev](https://arcade.dev/blog/smithery-is-now-part-of-arcade-dev)). The best-known independent attempt at exactly this product concluded that the connection layer was not a business.

Catalog size cannot rescue a new entrant either. The official MCP Registry held **53,553 active servers on 1 November 2026**; a full crawl found **63,364**; aggregator Glama claims **124,345**; skill counts range from **600 to 39,697** depending on whether prompts and agents are counted ([MCPpedia](https://mcppedia.com/blog/how-many-mcp-servers-are-there); [mcp-registry-census](https://github.com/zovo-one/mcp-registry-census); [mcptoplist](https://mcptoplist.com/observatory); [Skill Locker](https://skilllocker.ai/blog/state-of-claude-code-skills-2026)). The counts disagree by 2x because the definitions differ, and **56.2% of registry names carry a single version** — the long tail is publish-once artifacts, not maintained software ([MCPpedia](https://mcppedia.com/blog/how-many-mcp-servers-are-there)). A new store would enter with less inventory than four incumbents and no exclusive supply.

The cross-host premise dissolved at the protocol layer, which is the most durable way to lose an argument. **MCP Apps became the first official MCP extension in January 2026, co-authored by OpenAI and Anthropic maintainers** and supported by Claude, ChatGPT, VS Code, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Cursor and Goose ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-01-26-mcp-apps/)). Cross-host capability is now a property of the standard, which removes the need for a cross-host intermediary. What remains is admission: a third-party store that ships "install into Claude or ChatGPT" without passing host review is either blocked or falls back to a config file, returning the user to the exact friction the store promised to remove. Meanwhile OpenAI's developer terms let it "reject or remove an App at any time and for any reason," disclaim any placement or visibility guarantee, and add submission fees later ([OpenAI App Developer Terms](https://openai.com/policies/developer-apps-terms/)). A store whose inventory lives on someone else's protocol, in someone else's directory, with no contractual floor, is a feature in search of a company.

Monetization is the binding constraint, not demand. At launch, ChatGPT developers could monetize only by linking out to their own apps, and as of September 2026 OpenAI still had not announced store-like billing or revenue share ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-just-launched-an-app-store-inside-chatgpt-133049586.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openais-latest-features-take-direct-aim-at-the-app-store-model)). The GPT Store's promised revenue share stayed an invite-only pilot ([WIRED via Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/24/10/11/1033247/openais-gpt-store-has-left-some-developers-in-the-lurch)). On the paid side, the top-installed skills on one commercial marketplace recorded **116, 65, 49, 36 and 30 installs** ([Skill Locker](https://skilllocker.ai/blog/state-of-claude-code-skills-2026)). And **76% of consumers usually look for a free alternative rather than pay for an app** ([Yodel Mobile / NP Digital](https://adtechtoday.com/research-ai-influences-app-discovery-but-personal-recommendations-remain-twice-as-trusted)), while the shelf itself is drowning: App Store submissions roughly doubled to **560,000 in H1 2026** as downloads rose **2%**, and U.S. App Store consumer spending fell about **6% year on year** in Q2 2026, the first decline in a decade ([Business Model Analyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/ai-vibecoding-app-store-flood/); [OfficeChai / Sensor Tower](https://officechai.com/ai/even-as-number-of-apps-has-skyrocketed-us-spending-on-apples-app-store-has-fallen-for-first-time-in-a-decade/)).

The strongest counter-evidence deserves a fair hearing, because it is real: Anthropic reports **1 million skills published, nearly 280 million installs, and 70+ agent platforms** integrating the Agent Skills standard ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation)). Both facts can be true at once. Skills spread because the format is a free, open, GitHub-distributed commodity with no gatekeeper; that is precisely why a store cannot tax it. The install figure is also unsegmented by consumer versus developer, and "installed" is an undefined bar — bundled with a plugin, one CLI command, or actually used all count the same. Free distribution and absent monetization are not a market; they are a format.

## A trust badge on adversarial software is a liability, not a moat

"Safe" was the concept's most attractive word and its least defensible claim, because a static audit inspects what a server *declares*, while the attacks that matter are decided at runtime. The **Deadbugz** campaign served benign metadata for the first three tool calls, then switched to credential-seeking instructions and told the agent to hide the activity; it reached victims through **23 GitHub pull requests in 74 minutes** ([Pillar Security](https://www.pillar.security/blog/deadbugz-currently-active-mcp-supply-chain-campaign)). A malicious "compliance SDK" exfiltrated data in **under 90 seconds**, triggered by the `tools/list` handshake *before any user prompt*, and bypassed CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Defender, Sophos and DLP/CASB ([Morphisec](https://morphisec.com/mcp-attack-chain/)). A cloned Oura MCP server with fake forks and AI-generated contributor accounts distributed StealC through public registries ([Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/188135/ai/smartloader-hackers-clone-oura-mcp-project-to-spread-stealc-malware.html)). Nineteen malicious npm packages deployed their own MCP server and used embedded prompt injection to harvest SSH keys, cloud credentials and npm tokens ([Field Effect](https://fieldeffect.com/blog/typosquatting-campaign-sandworm-mode)).

The scanners a badge would be built on are already defeated. In June 2026, Trail of Bits bypassed ClawHub's malicious-skill detector, Cisco's skill scanner, **and all three scanners on skills.sh** ([Kipt](https://kipt.me/guides/agent-skills-marketplaces)). Tool poisoning succeeds at an average **36.5%** across model settings and up to **72.8%** against some real-world servers ([MCPTox, AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40895)). The supply base does not support a quality claim either: only **4.3% of MCP listings score 60/100 or better** on one independent evidence scale, two listings clear 80, and **77.5% have no readable license** ([MCPpedia](https://mcppedia.com/blog/how-many-mcp-servers-are-there)). The infrastructure has already been breached once at scale — a path traversal in Smithery's Docker build exposed a token granting code execution across **3,243 apps and 3,000+ hosted servers**, enabling API-key interception ([GitGuardian](https://blog.gitguardian.com/breaking-mcp-server-hosting/)).

Platform review does not close the gap, because it verifies declarations rather than behaviour: per-tool `readOnlyHint` and `destructiveHint` annotations, declared CSP domains, and a privacy policy, with **no pre-flight check built into the platforms**, so every fix costs a full review cycle ([Alpic / Beacon](https://alpic.ai/blog/introducing-beacon-what-dozens-of-chatgpt-app-store-submissions-taught-us)). The missing primitives are exactly the ones a consumer needs — digital signing, sandboxed execution, granular per-path permissions and reputation ([den.dev](https://den.dev/blog/vs-code-mcp-install-consent/)). Enterprise buyers already price this risk correctly: **64% cite security as the top obstacle to MCP adoption**, and only **22% have MCP-specific controls** ([Stacklok](https://stacklok.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/State-of-MCP-in-Software-2026%5FFINAL.pdf); [Postman](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261118374573/en/One-in-Four-Organizations-Now-Run-AI-Agents-on-APIs-in-Production-Postmans-2026-State-of-the-API-Report-Finds)). A consumer badge is an attestation about software the auditor cannot see into, produced by a party with less telemetry than the endpoint agent. One bypass becomes the headline; that is a liability claim with a marketing budget.

What survives from the original concept is not a consumer store. It is (a) a free metadata and curation feed that hosts ingest, (b) enterprise supply-chain assurance with runtime policy and audit evidence, where liability transfer is actually purchased, and (c) a vertical bundle sold on an outcome. None of those is a mass-appeal consumer app, and all three are occupied — Docker sells governance, Alpic sells pre-submission audits, Arcade sells the registry. Tested against the three conditions a consumer business needs — demand that is not a developer preference, a moat a host cannot copy, and a payer who is not the platform — the store fails all three. The pivot's market read was right and its unit of sale was wrong: a store sells capabilities one at a time to people who buy outcomes in bundles.

## Three concepts survive a mass-appeal test — only one has a structural moat

Scoring below is judgment, 1–5, on criteria chosen for this brief rather than for a developer tool: reach (a non-technical person gets value in under five minutes), strength of demand evidence, willingness to pay, defensibility against a platform owner, and feasibility for a small team. The ranking deliberately weights reach and habit first, because adoption in this market is decided by repeat use rather than by the quality of the argument.

| # | Concept | Reach | Demand | WTP | Defensibility | Feasibility | Total /25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Local-first private document assistant | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 | **20** |
| 2 | AI operator for micro-businesses | 3 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 3 | **19** |
| 3 | Creator artifact studio | 5 | 4 | 3 | 1 | 4 | **17** |
| — | Cross-host skills store (reference) | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 3 | **11** |

The desktop copilot, self-hosted assistant UI, consumer memory layer and skills store were cut. The copilot is a distribution play that loses to Raycast and to the OS vendors, and only **19% of consumers would switch their primary browser for better AI** ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/ai-browsers-are-a-successful-failure/)). Self-hosted UI is already won and free at **159k GitHub stars and 477 million Docker pulls** ([Open WebUI](https://openwebui.com/)). Memory became a native host feature after Meta acquired Limitless and disabled Rewind's screen and audio capture ([Limitless](https://www.limitless.ai/)). The store ranks last on every criterion that matters here.

**1. Local-first private document assistant.** This is the only concept where the incumbent is *structurally unable* to match the promise: a cloud assistant's economics depend on retaining and, on many tiers, training on the data, so "your files never leave and are never trained on" is a different business rather than a feature toggle. The demand signal is the gap between wanting the utility and distrusting the custodian — **90% worry about AI using their data without consent** in a self-selected reader survey, **76% of non-users cite privacy as an objection** in nationally representative data, and **64% of agent users have already connected an inbox** while **85% remain concerned and 42% have since reduced access** ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/press/2026/03/17/malwarebytes-survey-finds-90-of-people-dont-trust-ai-with-their-data); [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/); [Howdy](https://www.howdy.com/blog/ai-privacy-concern-statistics)). Crucially, **20% of agent users have caught an agent accessing data they had prohibited** — which makes citations and a revocable ledger retention features, not compliance theatre. Repositioned from "second brain" (a prosumer framing with a long abandonment record) to *household records* — receipts, warranties, school letters, medical letters, contracts, tax documents — it becomes a job every adult has and no one enjoys.

The primary risk is that privacy is a stated concern but not a paid benefit. Only **2.2%–4.5% of U.S. households pay for any AI chatbot**, the installed base that can run a good local model is "minuscule," and privacy is called "the weakest sales argument" by a practitioner who tracks the category ([PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/state-of-local-ai/2026-q4/state-of-local-ai-2026-q4-report.pdf)). The design answer is local-first *index*, cloud-optional *reasoning*: if the local model is visibly worse than free ChatGPT, no promise of custody survives. A second risk is indifference rather than competition — "ask my files" can be a utility used twice, not a habit.

**2. AI operator for micro-businesses.** This ranks second only because the money is real. n8n reached roughly **$100M ARR, 1.7 million monthly active builders and 1,400+ enterprise customers**, with **over 80% of 2025 workflows involving AI agents**, and its own Chat Hub exists precisely because the canvas is still too hard for non-technical staff ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/n8n/)). Anthropic's small-business launch names the jobs that actually hurt — payroll planning, month-end close, invoice chasing, margin analysis, tax-season prep, contract review, lead triage — and concedes that use "often stops at the chat window" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-small-business)). **48% of AI users have earned money with AI**, 18% through freelancing, which turns a $20–100 monthly fee into a cost of goods rather than a discretionary subscription ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)).

The primary risk is that Anthropic is giving 15 pre-built workflows and 15 skills away free, bundled with per-seat distribution and a workshop tour, and that this segment carries the highest trust barrier of any consumer-adjacent group — half name data security as their single biggest hesitation. Integration depth is also a support business, not a software business. A new entrant must pick one trade (contractors, clinics, e-commerce sellers) and own its compliance surface, or it will lose to a free bundle with better distribution.

**3. Creator artifact studio.** The reach case is the best of any concept: **43% of consumers say image creation is what they would try next and 40% say writing**, **53% already create images**, **22% have earned money through content creation**, and a shareable artifact is the only growth loop that matches how this market spreads — word of mouth at **38% of discovery and 84% of trust** ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/); [Yodel Mobile / NP Digital](https://adtechtoday.com/research-ai-influences-app-discovery-but-personal-recommendations-remain-twice-as-trusted)). A finished deck, listing or video is a 15-second demo a friend can send.

The primary risk is that there is no moat at all. The product is a UI over rented models, in a category ChatGPT, Gemini and Canva bundle for free, with a user base that churns when the format moves. It earns its place on this list because reach and willingness to earn are the two things the skills store lacked, not because it is defensible.

## The MVP is one folder, one citation, and a kill switch

The strongest concept should ship as a single-user macOS app (Apple Silicon, Tauri or native), not a platform. Scope: watch `~/Documents`, `~/Desktop` and `~/Downloads`, accept one optional Obsidian vault path, and offer one optional read-only Gmail connection — one high-value connector, never a catalog. Index locally into SQLite with local embeddings, re-index incrementally, show a "why this matched" snippet per hit. Answers must carry citations to file and page or line, a "show your work" drawer, and an explicit "I don't know" when retrieval is weak. Actions are read-only plus draft: summarize, find, compare, draft — never send, pay, or write without a confirm step, and every action lands in an activity ledger with before and after. Models default to Ollama or LM Studio auto-detect, with an optional OpenAI or Anthropic key and a per-request toggle that previews exactly what would leave the machine. Trust surface: plain-language permission cards, one-click revoke, an egress log, no telemetry by default, signed releases and a public threat model. Explicit non-goals: marketplace, plugins, MCP, mobile, multi-user, agentic purchasing. Price at **$10/month**, inside the modal $10–20 band and below the $25 ceiling that 40% of willing payers refuse to cross ([Rev](https://www.rev.com/blog/how-much-is-ai-worth); [Empirical Intelligence](https://empiricalintel.com/reports/consumers-ai.pdf)).

Before writing the app, run the cheapest falsification available: manually index folders for 30 non-technical people over two weeks and count how many ask a second question in week two. That is a two-week test of the only assumption that matters — whether this becomes a habit — and it costs less than the onboarding screen.

The kill criteria are ordered, and each one tests a different load-bearing assumption. First, **time to first cited answer under five minutes from install**; if a non-technical tester cannot get there unaided, the product is a developer tool with a consumer price. Second, **at least 40% of day-seven actives connect a second source by day 14**; below 15%, the "more context makes it valuable" hypothesis is dead. Third, **at least 60% of answers carry an accepted citation**; below 35%, retrieval is not good enough to charge for. Fourth, **at least 25% of activated users are still using it weekly at day 30**; below 10%, it is a one-off utility and no pricing fixes that. Fifth, **at least 5% free-to-paid at $10/month by day 90 across at least 200 activated users**; below 2%, the privacy premium is falsified and the correct move is to pivot to the micro-business operator, not to resurrect the store. Any confirmed instance of data leaving the machine without an explicit request stops the roadmap and triggers an external audit. And a standing platform check: if Apple or Microsoft ships a credible on-device "your files, no training" assistant with default distribution before v1, the structural wedge is gone and the concept should be re-scored rather than defended.

Two calibration caveats belong with the numbers. The paid-conversion thresholds are design judgments, not observed rates — no public benchmark exists for free-to-paid conversion in local-first AI. And the willingness-to-pay data has a credibility problem worth naming: one widely cited survey puts AI payers at **55% of users, up from 3% a year earlier**, a 52-point swing that is implausible as behaviour change and probably reflects a changed question; treat the level as directional and the trend as unusable ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)).

## Conclusion

The pivot's error is the unit of sale, not the market. A store assumes consumers shop for capabilities and will pay a toll to a trusted middleman; the evidence says they buy outcomes, already pay the assistant subscription, and route discovery through people they know. That is why the same set of facts that kills the store elevates a document assistant: the scarce thing in consumer AI is not access to capability but permission to be trusted with context, and only a product that refuses to hold that context can sell the promise credibly.

The ranking also carries an uncomfortable implication for how to read it. The top two concepts are not really mass-market consumer products — one is a prosumer desktop app, the other a small-business tool — and that is the finding, not a flaw in the list. Consumer AI's mass market has already been monetized once, by the $20-a-month assistant subscription, and everything above it either rides a host's distribution or is justified by income the user earns. Any future concept that cannot answer "who bundles this, or what does it earn the user" should be treated as a feature, not a company — which is exactly the test the skills store failed.
